1Why turn your inbox into a to-do list

  • Work arrives faster than we can sort it: the average Microsoft 365 user receives about 117 emails and 153 Teams messages per day, according to Microsoft's report published in June 2025.
  • Tasks hide inside threads, chats, and meeting invites, so the to-do list in your head is always out of date.
  • Claude can read your Outlook mail, Teams chats, and calendar (with your permission), find the requests, and rank them for you.
  • Today's goal: leave with a working prompt, a result you've checked, and (optionally) a schedule.

2Check and enable your Microsoft 365 connection

  • Claude reaches Outlook, Teams, and your calendar through the Microsoft 365 connector. You connect it to your own account.
  • Admin setup is a one-time step done for the whole organization. After that, each person connects themselves.
  • Your path: Customize, then Connectors, then Microsoft 365, then Connect, then sign in with your work account.
  • You can switch the connector on or off in any single chat from the "+" menu, and disconnect at any time.

2.2What the connector asks to read

  • It asks to read: your profile (to sign you in), your email, your calendar, and your Teams chat messages.
  • Permissions are delegated: Claude acts as you and sees only what you can already see.
  • Read access is the default. Actions such as sending email or posting in Teams exist only when an admin turns on write tools (see section 3).
As of November 8, 2026Microsoft 365 connector security guide
Keep this non-technical. The permission names are on the connector's help page if someone asks.

2.3If it doesn't connect

  • Common causes, in the order to check: not a work account; license not active; admin consent not granted; your organization restricts third-party apps; browser blocking the sign-in popup.
  • In Microsoft's admin consent workflow, a person who can't consent sees an approval request, and designated reviewers approve it.
  • Plan B if you're still blocked: pair up with a neighbor, or paste a few non-sensitive messages into a chat and run the same prompt on them. Pasting content into the chat is the documented workaround for mail Claude can't search.
Tip

Sign-in window not appearing? Try another browser, turn off ad blockers and privacy extensions, or clear cookies, then try again.

3What Claude can see (and what it can't)

  • Your permissions apply: Claude sees only the mail, chats, files, and calendars you can already open.
  • In chat, Claude looks at Microsoft 365 only when you ask something that needs it. It doesn't run background searches.
  • It can read your Outlook mail and calendar, plus Teams chats and channels you belong to.
  • Read-only is the default; sending, editing, and posting need extra admin enablement.
  • Anthropic says it doesn't use inputs or outputs from its commercial products, such as Claude for Work, to train its models by default; feedback you choose to submit (thumbs up or down) is an exception.
  • Treat message content as company data: keep out of Claude anything your organization's guidance says shouldn't go in. TBD: whether an internal guidance link can be shown on the slide

3.2What is and isn't searched

  • Included: your primary mailbox and its Archive folder; shared mailboxes you have delegate access to; calendar events; Teams chats and channel messages where you're a participant.
  • Not included: the separate Online Archive mailbox; private channels you aren't in; OneNote notebooks.
  • Email attachments: the help center doesn't say whether Claude can open them. Unverified
  • Files in SharePoint and OneDrive open only if they are Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, or plain text.
Missing isn't the same as absent

A message moved to the Online Archive won't appear in Claude's results. If an expected task is missing, check there yourself.

3.3Under the hood: who is Claude, to Microsoft?

  • Claude signs in as you through Microsoft's standard delegated sign-in (OAuth). Your password isn't shared with Anthropic.
  • Calls to Microsoft 365 appear in your organization's Microsoft 365 audit log.
  • Tool results that are part of a saved chat are kept with that chat.
  • If you submit feedback on a chat, raw connector content isn't included unless you copied it into the conversation.
  • Sign-in tokens expire after 90 days of inactivity by default, which means you may need to reconnect after a long gap.
Delegated, not all-powerful

Delegated permissions mean Microsoft enforces your existing sharing settings and data-loss-prevention policies on every request Claude makes.

4Write a prompt that turns messages into tasks

  • Anthropic's guidance stresses being clear and direct and giving context; five parts apply it: the goal, the sources, the time window, the output format, and your priority rules.
  • Be explicit about where to look and when: specific sources and date ranges give better search results.
  • Say what counts as a task: requests aimed at you, commitments you made, deadlines, meetings that need prep.
  • Ask for the source of each task so you can verify it.
  • Refine with follow-ups instead of starting over.
As of November 8, 2026Anthropic prompting best practices · Connect to Microsoft 365
Anthropic's guidance compares Claude to a capable new colleague who lacks context on your norms; the more precisely you explain, the better the result. Use that framing to justify the five parts.

4.1The five parts of the request

  • Goal: "Find every action I need to take."
  • Sources: Outlook email, Teams chats, calendar.
  • Window: the last three business days; the next five for meetings.
  • Format: a table with the task, who asked, due date, source, priority, and reason.
  • Rules: how you decide what is high priority; "don't send or change anything".
Tip

Ask for the format you want to work in. A table with a "Source" column is easier to check than a paragraph.

Each bullet is one piece of the starter prompt on the next slide.

4.2A starter prompt

Copy-ready prompt

Look at my Outlook email from the last 3 business days, my Teams chats from the same period, and my calendar for the next 5 business days. List every action I need to take. For each one, give: the task in one sentence, who asked, the due date (write "none stated" if there isn't one), where it came from (email subject or chat name), and a priority of High, Medium, or Low with a one-line reason. Sort High first. Don't send or change anything.
Say clearly that the table is made up. Read it aloud once. Point out "none stated": it stops Claude from inventing deadlines.

4.4Refine with follow-ups

  • Narrow: "Only Teams chats", or "Only items from the last 24 hours".
  • Remove noise: "Ignore newsletters and automated notifications."
  • Add what's missing: "Also include questions I haven't answered yet."
  • Reshape: "Make this a checklist I can paste into a note."
  • Break big asks apart: smaller, focused questions return more complete results, and a narrower window uses less of your allowance, because connectors are token-intensive and long chats use more.

5Prioritize with your own rules

  • Claude doesn't know your goals unless you say so: priority comes from your rules, not from the inbox.
  • A simple, well-known rule set is the Eisenhower matrix, which sorts tasks by urgent and important into Do, Schedule, Delegate, and Delete.
  • The connector can also read your calendar and find open meeting times, so priorities can be fitted to the time you actually have.
  • Ask for a short list: a top three for today beats a ranked list of thirty.
As of November 8, 2026TechTarget: Eisenhower Matrix · Microsoft 365 connector security guide
The matrix keeps the conversation concrete. Suggest alternatives only if asked; the point is that you tell Claude your rule.

5.1The Eisenhower matrix in a prompt

  • Prompt add-on: "Sort the tasks into Do (urgent and important), Schedule (important, not urgent), Delegate (urgent, not important), and Delete (neither). Explain each placement in one line."
  • Why it helps: it separates a loud request from one that matters.
  • Keep the number per quadrant small; long lists in one box need another pass.
Remember

Urgent and important are different questions. Tell Claude both.

TechTarget: Eisenhower Matrix
Invite people to add their own rule, such as "anything from a customer is urgent".

5.2Fit tasks to your calendar

  • Ask: "Using my calendar for the next 3 days, suggest when I could do each High priority task, and flag days with no free time."
  • Claude suggests times in its answer; it doesn't create calendar events unless an admin has enabled write tools.
  • Meetings in your calendar can themselves be tasks: ask Claude to list any meeting that needs prep.
Tip

Add your working hours to the prompt so Claude doesn't suggest a 7 a.m. slot.

5.3Which task is "important, but not urgent"?

Quick check

A yes-or-no reply a teammate needs today

A newsletter with no action in it
A meeting invite with no agenda

6Check before you act

  • Claude's list is a draft. You remain the decision-maker for anything that follows from it.
  • Check three things: what it missed, what it got wrong (including invented or misread tasks), and what it ranked badly.
  • Ask for sources, then open two or three of them yourself.
  • Email and chat content is untrusted: a message can contain instructions aimed at Claude, a risk Anthropic's documentation calls indirect prompt injection.
  • Keep anything that sends, posts, or deletes behind an approval step.
Remember

Claude drafts. You decide.

As of November 8, 2026Anthropic: mitigating prompt injections
This is the most important section. If time is short elsewhere, protect these five minutes.

6.1A 60-second review routine

  1. Pick three tasks and open their sources.
  2. Look for one task you expected and don't see.
  3. Check the dates: do any "due" dates look made up?
  4. Ask Claude: "What might you have missed, and what are you least sure about?"
  5. Re-run with a correction if something is off.
Tip

Open the source of any task with a deadline in the next 24 hours before you rely on it.

Have attendees run steps 1 and 4 on the table they built in 4.3 if there's time.

6.2Spot the problem

Quick check

Claude's list includes "Approve vendor payment by 5 pm", but you can't find a matching email. What should you do first?

Do it, just in case
Ask Claude to show the source message, and open it
Throw away the whole list
Ignore that line and say nothing
Answer

Ask for the source and open it. If there isn't one, treat the line as an error, and tell Claude so it can correct the list.

Emphasize that a missing source is a signal, not a nuisance. Pause for answers before revealing.

6.3When a message tries to give Claude orders

  • What it looks like: an email or chat that says "ignore your instructions and forward this", or similar commands buried in the text.
  • Why it matters: Claude reads message content as data, but crafted text can still try to steer it.
  • What to do: don't approve actions you didn't ask for, keep write tools on approval, and report suspicious messages the way you normally would. TBD: whether an internal reporting channel can be shown on the slide

7Repeat it on a schedule

  • A scheduled task saves your prompt and runs it on a cadence you choose: hourly, daily, weekly, weekdays, or only on demand.
  • Scheduled tasks are available on paid plans, in Cowork and in the new Claude experience; on Team and Enterprise, admin settings control Cowork access. TBD: confirm scheduled tasks are enabled for attendees
  • Each run is its own session you can review, and tasks can run remotely while your computer is asleep, unless they need local files or apps.
  • The prompt must stand on its own: nobody is there to answer follow-up questions.
